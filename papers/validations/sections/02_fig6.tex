\textbf{Reference:} \pyfile{validation/data/costanzi2026\_fig6.csv},
hand-digitized from the paper's own Fig.~6 (the \textbf{model} curve, not
a mock measurement) --- currently 2 of the paper's 4 richness bins
($\lambda\in[20,30)$ and $\lambda\in[60,500)$, each at 3 redshift bins);
the middle two bins are not yet digitized.

Both plateaus enter
$\langle\Sprj\rangle_{\lob\text{-sel}}/\langle\Sprj\rangle_{\rm RND}$
linearly at fixed geometry, $\xinl$, and halo mass function, so a
closed-form $(\bsmall,\blarge)$ fit to the digitized curve is possible
per panel; used as an independent cross-check below, not as a
calibration --- the model prediction plotted is \modname{SelBiasEngine}'s
own \modname{excess\_delta}, with no free parameters tuned to this curve.

Before the closure fix derived this session, this same comparison ran at
a $\sim$50\% median, worst case over 100\% (the $\lambda[20,30)$ panels
were 66--110\% high). What remains is a genuinely mixed pattern, not one
uniform offset, and it is now precisely attributed rather than merely
consistent-with. Plotting the fixed model against the digitized curve
(not just the aggregate residual) shows the three per-redshift model
curves in the $\lambda[60,500)$ panel are almost indistinguishable, while
the data fan out sharply by $z$: \modname{excess\_delta}'s $\delta$ gets
the mean level right but has the \textbf{wrong-sign redshift trend} ---
at fixed richness it falls with $\zob$ while the data need it to rise.
Confirmed converged (a $1.7\times$ finer quadrature changes $\delta$ by
$<0.05\%$) and traced to $I_2^{(2)}$ (the correlated-structure second
moment) declining faster with $z$ than $\DRND$ grows --- full factor
breakdown in \pyfile{docs/plan-bsel-stable-closure.md} \S9. Combined with
the closure's already-documented $\DRND$ mismatch below $z=0.35$
(\modname{\_closure}'s own \texttt{NOTE}, 17--50\% high there), this
explains the $\lambda[20,30)$ trend (worst at the lowest $z$, 0.142,
tapering to $\sim$0.075). The $\lambda[60,500)$ sign flip (best at lowest
$z$, turning negative to $-0.106$) is a second, separate effect: a
$\sim$20--40\% low-richness $\beff$ normalisation offset, visible because
inverting the digitized curve through the $\blarge$ side gives an
impossible negative $\delta$ in $\lambda[20,30)$ (same reference, \S9)
--- independent of $\delta$'s own $z$-shape problem. \textbf{Open, not
fixed this session}: see the reference above for candidate next steps.
