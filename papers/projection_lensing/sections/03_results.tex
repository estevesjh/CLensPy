In the two-halo regime, $R > 3\,h^{-1}$cMpc, the model reproduces the
mock's selected-to-random ratio in every bin
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ratio}, Table~\ref{tab:bins}): the per-bin maximum
absolute deviation ranges from \MinTwoHaloResid{} to \MaxTwoHaloResid{},
with a median across bins of \MedTwoHaloResid{}. This is the regime the
observable is designed for --- the ratio cancels the common halo budget,
and what remains tests the $b\,\bsel\,\xinl$ weighting of the correlated
channel. The absolute mass-map column $\langle\Stot\rangle$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:absolute})
agrees to \LegCMaxDev{} there; the residual offset is the
Tinker-versus-simulation halo budget entering both channels alike, which
is exactly what the ratio removes.

The closure's prediction of the mean projected richness boost deserves
separate mention. $\Delta^{\rm prj}_{\rm RND} = P_1 + \beff I_2$ is
built entirely from the projection-kernel operators and the bin
$\beff$ --- no fit to the mock --- and it matches the measured
$\langle\lob - \ltr\rangle$ to \DeltaPrjAgreeMin--\DeltaPrjAgreeMax{}
across all \NBins{} bins (Table~\ref{tab:bins}). The operator machinery
of the selection model is therefore validated independently of the
$b_{\rm small}$ calibration.

\subsection{The inner region: closure equivalence and the
marginalisation bracket}
\label{sec:inner}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.66\textwidth]{fig_inner_study.pdf}
  \caption{The $b_{\rm small}$ marginalisation bracket at the reference
  bin ($\lob \in [20,30)$, $z \in [0.35,0.50)$): the mock ratio against
  the same closure fed four different $\ltr$ marginalisations. The
  fitted inner plateau is $b_{\rm small} = \BsmallFit{}$.}
  \label{fig:inner}
\end{figure}

Inside $\sim 2\Rlam$ the comparison is reported unscored: the model
ratio overshoots the mock by up to \MaxInnerResid{} there. Transcribing
the Costanzi notebook's closure term by term shows its background and
normalisation are \emph{algebraically identical} to ours ---
$\bar\Delta_{\rm bkg} = P_1 + b_{\rm large} I_1$ and the two-halo
normalisation $I_2 - I_1$ --- so the entire inner plateau hinges on the
$\ltr$ marginalisation of $b_{\rm small}(\ltr)$, and the notebook does
not define the marginalisation it feeds its $\theta$-grid interpolator
(the function is used but absent). Four defensible choices bracket the
mock (Fig.~\ref{fig:inner}): the DES~Y3 EMG kernel gives
$b_{\rm small} = \BsmallYthree{}$ (too high --- its tail, calibrated on
SDSS injections, overstates $\langle\lob - \ltr\rangle$ against this
unpercolated mock); the self-consistent exponential kernel weighted by
the $n(\ltr)$ prior gives \BsmallSelf{} (far too high --- the steep
prior under the flat exponential tail drives the marginal boost to
three times the kernel's own mean); the closure evaluated at its own
mean boost $\Delta_{\rm RND}$ returns exactly $b_{\rm small} = \beff =
\BsmallPoint{}$ (too low --- an algebraic identity of the closure,
$\Delta_{\rm RND} = P_1 + \beff I_2$, and a useful internal
consistency check). The mock-implied plateau, fitted on
$R < 1\,h^{-1}$cMpc, is $b_{\rm small} = \BsmallFit{}$: about
$2.5\,\beff$, between the point evaluation and the EMG
marginalisation. With that single calibrated scalar, the model tracks
the mock ratio to within \FitMaxPull{}$\sigma$ at \emph{every}
radius (mean pull \FitMeanPull{}$\sigma$, Fig.~\ref{fig:inner}) ---
plateau, sigmoid transition, and two-halo tail all at once, so the
closure's shape needs no retuning, only its $\ltr$ marginalisation.
Matching that from first principles requires the boost-weighted
$P(\ltr \mid \lob)$ of the mock itself --- a calibration of the
richness kernel, not a bug fix; the fitted value is recorded here as
the target any such calibration must hit.
