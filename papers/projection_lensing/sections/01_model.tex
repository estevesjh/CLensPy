\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

A cluster selected at observed richness $\lob$ preferentially sits behind
extra line-of-sight structure --- the same projections that boosted its
richness lens the sources behind it. The stacked surface density around a
$\lob$-selected sample therefore carries, beyond the cluster's own halo, a
projected two-halo term. The two-halo term is the correlated excess
\emph{above} the mean matter column --- it carries no background; this is
the \modname{cluster\_toolkit} $\Sigma_{2h}$ convention, and it is what a
random-point-subtracted measurement contains. Around a cluster observed
at $(\lob, \zob)$, with $\theta$ the angular offset of a line-of-sight
neighbour and $\chi_o = \chi(\zob)$ the comoving distance,

\begin{equation}
\Sprj(R) \;=\; \int d\theta\, 2\pi\sin\theta\;
  \bsel(\theta) \sum_M \wcl(\theta, M)\,
  \Smis\!\big(R,\, \theta\chi_o \mid M\big),
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
where $\Smis$ is the azimuth-averaged surface density of an NFW halo
offset by $R_\theta = \theta\chi_o$, and the redshift weight is
\begin{equation}
\wcl(\theta, M) = \int dz\;{\rm common}(z)\;
    \xinl\!\big(|d\chi|(z,\theta),\, \zob\big)\;
    n(M, z)\, b(M, z)\; m_{\rm cl}(\theta, z),
\qquad
{\rm common}(z) = \frac{dV}{d\Omega\,dz}\; w_{\rm los}(z; \zob),
\label{eq:wcl}
\end{equation}
with $n(M,z)$ the Tinker (2008) mass function, $b(M,z)$ the Tinker (2010)
bias, $\xinl$ the halofit correlation function, and $|d\chi|$ the exact
chord $d\chi^2 = \chi_z^2 + \chi_o^2 - 2\chi_z\chi_o\cos\theta$. The
measure $2\pi\sin\theta\,d\theta$ is an exact angular integral on the
sphere; there is no Limber approximation and no Bessel transform in this
observable. $\bsel(\theta)$ multiplies this correlated integrand alone.

A raw projected \emph{mass map} --- the mock's per-halo columns, or any
stack that has not been random-point subtracted --- additionally contains
the mean background column, the ``1'' of the halo-model bracket
$[1 + b\,\bsel\,\xinl]$. That piece is kept as the separate \blk{rnd}
channel,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{\rm bkg}(R) = \int d\theta\, 2\pi\sin\theta
  \sum_M \wrnd(\theta, M)\, \Smis\!\big(R,\, \theta\chi_o \mid M\big),
\qquad
\wrnd = \int dz\;{\rm common}(z)\, n(M, z)\, m_{\rm rnd},
\label{eq:wrnd}
\end{equation}
uniform in $R$ and blind to the selection; a raw mass map is the sum
$\Stot = \Sbkg + \Sprj$ (Section~\ref{sec:mock}).

Halo exclusion is applied as a \emph{counter term} (the Costanzi
convention): the cluster occupies its own volume, so inside the chord
ball $|d\chi| < \Rexcl = \Rlam(\lob)(1+\zob)$ the correlated integrand
is set to $-1$, cancelling the background's $+1$ exactly. The total
vanishes in the ball --- the same total as deleting the neighbours, as
the mock does --- but the bookkeeping matters: $\Sbkg$ stays strictly
uniform, and the exclusion hole (a $0.6\%$ suppression at $R \to 0$,
gone by $R \approx 2$~cMpc) is carried by $\Sprj$, where a
random-point-subtracted measurement keeps it. Placing the
``1'' inside the master equation --- as the spec this implementation
transcribed originally does --- invites exactly the bookkeeping error
this validation chased: a background-carrying model against a
background-free theory quantity.

The exclusion masks $m_{\rm rnd}, m_{\rm cl}$ implement halo exclusion at
$\Rexcl = \Rlam(\lob)(1+\zob)$ comoving, in two named semantics: the
production form masks the correlated channel only
($m_{\rm cl} = \mathbb 1[|d\chi| > \Rexcl]$, $m_{\rm rnd} = 1$), while the
\emph{ball} form removes both channels inside the chord ball --- which is
what a halo catalogue with the target's neighbours deleted actually does,
and is the convention of the mock in Section~\ref{sec:mock}.

\subsection{The excess $\DSprj$ is its own integral}

The excess functional acts only on the radial argument and commutes with
the outer $(\theta, z, M)$ integrals, so
\begin{equation}
\DSprj(R) \;\propto\; \int dz \int dM \int d\theta\;\ldots\;
\DSmis\!\big(R,\, \theta\chi_o \mid M\big):
\label{eq:kernelswap}
\end{equation}
the same operator with the kernel swap $\Smis \to \DSmis$, never a
numerical reconstruction from a tabulated $\Sprj$. The signed negative
lobe of $\DSmis$ at $R_\theta > R$ is load-bearing: mass conservation of
the azimuthal average gives $\int d^2s\, \DSmis(R, s) = 0$ exactly, so
the excess annihilates the uniform \blk{rnd} channel --- the model form
of the random-point subtraction --- and only the correlated channel
survives.

\subsection{The effective and the selection-affected bias}

The population bias of the bin enters twice. First as the bin average
\begin{equation}
\beff \;=\; \frac{N[b]}{N[1]}
\;=\; \frac{\int dM\,dz\; W_{ij}(M, z)\; b(M, z)}{\int dM\,dz\; W_{ij}},
\label{eq:beff}
\end{equation}
computed by the number-counts operator \modname{ClusterCounts.average}
with the full selection weight $W_{ij}$ (mass function, volume, richness
and redshift selection). Second through the selection-affected bias
$\bsel(\theta)$ of the \modname{SelBiasEngine} closure: a sigmoid between
an inner plateau $b_{\rm small}$ (the linear inversion of the observed
richness excess) and an outer plateau
$b_{\rm large} = \beff[1 + 0.13\,\delta^{\rm prj}]$, with the bin-averaged
$\beff$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:beff} threaded into the closure in place of the
engine's internal fixed-$\lob$ average.

\subsection{Numerics}

The $\theta$ integral of Eq.~\eqref{eq:master} cannot be done pointwise:
as a function of the offset $s = \theta\chi_o$ at fixed $R$, the kernel
$\Smis(R, s)$ is a ring of width $\sim r_s$ at $s \approx R$ --- the
azimuthal average passes through the halo's cusp --- and resolving it on
a log grid would take $\sim\!10^4$ nodes. Each $\theta$ cell
$[s_1, s_2]$ is instead integrated \emph{exactly}, in three steps. The
azimuthal average
\begin{equation}
\Smis(R, s) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\oint d\varphi\;
  \Sigma\big(\sqrt{R^2 + s^2 - 2Rs\cos\varphi}\big)
\label{eq:smisdef}
\end{equation}
is symmetric under $R \leftrightarrow s$, so the cell integral
$\int_{s_1}^{s_2} 2\pi s\, \Smis(R, s)\, ds = \int_{s_1}^{s_2} 2\pi s\,
\Smis(s, R)\, ds$ is the mass of the halo, \emph{offset by} $R$,
enclosed in the annulus --- a difference of two enclosed masses,
$M_{\rm mis}(<\!s_2 \mid R) - M_{\rm mis}(<\!s_1 \mid R)$. Each enclosed
mass is $\pi s^2$ times the offset aperture mean
$\bar\Sigma_{\rm mis}(<\!s \mid R)$, and the aperture mean is by
definition the sum $\Smis + \DSmis$, both already tabulated:
\begin{equation}
\int_{s_1}^{s_2} 2\pi s\,\Smis(R, s)\, ds
= \Big[\pi s^2\, \bar\Sigma_{\rm mis}(<\!s \mid R)\Big]_{s_1}^{s_2},
\qquad
\bar\Sigma_{\rm mis} = \Sigma_0\big(\hat\Sigma_{\rm mis}
+ \widehat{\DSmis}\big)\big(s/r_s,\, R/r_s\big),
\label{eq:cellmass}
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma_0 = 2 r_s \rho_s$ the NFW surface-density scale and the
hatted functions the dimensionless miscentered-NFW table entries at
aperture $s/r_s$ and offset $R/r_s$. No quadrature crosses the ring at
all. The $\DSmis$ kernel splits into a smooth aperture-mean term
(trapezoidal on the cell edges) minus the same exact ring. When a halo-centric truncation $r_{\rm trunc}$ is
requested (the mock samples its particles only to $30\,h^{-1}$cMpc of
each halo), the truncated tail $\Sigma(u > r_{\rm trunc})$ --- smooth,
since $r_{\rm trunc} \gg r_s$ --- is subtracted by direct quadrature;
masking the cell mass by the ring's arc-length fraction instead discards
the cusp-concentrated mass and over-truncates by tens of percent at the
outermost radii.

The redshift integrals of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:wcl} and \eqref{eq:wrnd} are,
at fixed $\theta$, the line-of-sight parametrization of a two-sided
truncated \emph{Abel transform} of $\xinl$: with
$u = |\Delta\chi_\parallel|$, the flat-sky chord gives
$\int du\, \xinl(\sqrt{u^2 + R_\theta^2}) =
2\int_{R_\theta}^{r_{\max}} \xinl(r)\, r\, dr / \sqrt{r^2 - R_\theta^2}$
--- the $r = R\cosh u$ substitution that removes the Abel edge
singularity is already built into the parametrization, and the same
substitution underlies every $\xi \to \Sigma$ routine in
\modname{clenspy.utils.integrate}. The exclusion ball is an exact inner
truncation of that transform. A dedicated numerical audit (flat-sky
chord error $\le 1.4\%$ at the grid's outer corner; thin-window weight
variations quantified; convergence scans against an $8\times$ finer
reference) found no singularity to protect against; the one genuine
non-smoothness is the exclusion indicator's crossing, pinned by
splitting each Gauss--Legendre side at $|\Delta\chi| = \Rexcl$, which
bounds the channel quadrature error at $2.6\times10^{-3}$ at the worst
radius.
